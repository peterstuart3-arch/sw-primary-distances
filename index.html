How to read this

“Last distance offered” is how far from the school the furthest child admitted on distance lived. It changes every year and is not a fixed catchment. Siblings, looked-after children, faith places and priority areas are offered before distance, so the figure only tells you about the distance criterion.

By default each school is judged across every year published. Safe means you’d have been inside the last distance (or everyone got in) in every year. Mixed means inside in some years but not others. Just inside means inside every year but by under 100 m in at least one, which is within the tool’s margin of error. Unlikely means outside in every year. One good year on its own is a weak signal, so the card flags schools with only one year of data. Pick a single year from the menu to see that year alone.

Straight-line distances are measured here from your postcode centre to the school’s postcode centre. Councils measure from your exact address point to a gate, entrance or site centre, so expect differences of up to about 100 m. So a school where you’re within 100 m of the cut-off is marked “Just inside” or “Just outside”: too close to call either way.

Walking distances use OpenStreetMap footpaths, which won’t match a council’s own road and footpath network exactly. On the map, walking-route schools show a dashed circle: the true walking area always sits inside it.

Ofsted no longer gives one overall grade. Schools inspected before September 2024 keep their old grade (Outstanding to Inadequate). Those inspected from September 2024 to November 2025 have area grades only. Since November 2025 schools get a report card rating six areas from Exceptional to Urgent improvement. Short “ungraded” inspections only confirm whether a school is still at its previous grade. Ofsted data is as at 31 August 2026.

Data: council allocation tables, offer-day figures, gathered October 2026. Always check the school’s current admissions policy and the council’s own figures before applying.
